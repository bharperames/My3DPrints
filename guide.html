Bambu Lab P2S · 0.4 nozzle · Textured PEI · AMS

P2S Field Guide

Working guidelines for material choice, geometry risk, slicer recipes, and building portable 3MF project files — distilled from real prints, real failures, and measured numbers on this machine.

01

Pick the material from what the part does

Material tracks the job of the joint and the surface, not general reputation. PLA is the default for anything precise, interlocking, or decorative; PETG earns its place on parts that flex repeatedly or live outdoors.

JobMaterialWhy
Print-in-place, captive & passthrough fidgets, spinning/sliding fitsPLA BasicStiff, slick, clean gaps, no strings welding parts together
Decorative / display partsPLA Silk / MatteFinish over strength — silk is the weakest PLA along layers
Snap-fits cycled repeatedly; clips under sustained loadPETGFlexes without stress-whitening or cracking like PLA
Outdoor, heat (car), tough bracketsPETGUV/heat tolerance, layer adhesion, toughness
Continuous overhang flares, domes, tall thin sculpturePLAPETG curls on sustained ≥35° overhangs → nozzle strikes
Multi-color platesOne material onlyPLA and PETG don't bond and want different temps — never mix in a print
  • PLA variants: Basic is the workhorse for mechanisms. Matte hides layer lines but is grippy (bad for rubbing surfaces). Silk is the most brittle — never for flexing features. Bambu Silk+ improves on old silk but still sits below Basic.
  • PETG variants: Bambu PETG HF is formulated for less stringing at speed — prefer it over Generic/Basic when buying.
  • Door policy: PLA — crack the door on long prints once chamber passes ~35 °C (≤30 °C is fine). PETG — closed; warmth helps layer adhesion.
02

Check the geometry before slicing

Most "bad print" failures are geometry risks that were visible before the first layer. Four numbers predict them:

≥ 4:1Height ÷ base half-width lever ratio → elevated knock-over risk. Brim becomes mandatory (also at any height ≥150 mm).
> 50°Wall angle from vertical that needs support. Below ~45–50° prints clean with a 0.4 nozzle.
≥ 35°A continuous flare at this angle around the perimeter curls at the edges (worse in PETG) — curl is what the nozzle strikes.
islandsFirst-layer contact is about islands, not area. Each separate island must hold its own tower; gate on the smallest one, and brim when there are many.

Knock-over mechanics, in one line: the printer runs open-loop — curled edges and part sway put plastic where the G-code doesn't expect it, the nozzle hits it, and everything above that layer is spaghetti.

Also check the mesh: watertight, no non-manifold edges. The common export defect is a zero-thickness duplicate-face flap — slicers usually auto-repair it silently, but fix the file to remove the gamble.

Multi-color (AMS)

Two modes with wildly different costs — pick deliberately:

ModeUse whenCost
By object — each object completes fully, then the nextEach object is a single colorOne filament change per object. Needs toolhead clearance: 72 mm radius between parts on the P2S, X-rod 32.5 mm above nozzle — always re-Arrange after switching printers
By layer — colors change within layersColors mix within one objectA change every layer: prime tower + flush waste. The same plate measured 1h30m by-object vs 16h42m by-layer

Flush goes to the prime tower by default; flush_into_support / infill recovers some waste. By-object barely needs a prime tower at all.

PETG handling

  • Dry first, tune second — 55–65 °C for 6–8 h; store with desiccant. Popping sounds, fuzz, rough matte surfaces = wet filament, not bad settings. "New roll" does not guarantee dry.
  • Temperature at the low end of the range — ooze scales strongly with temp. Strings? Drop 10 °C before touching anything else.
  • Fine wisps that brush off are normal for PETG; zero-string PLA-style results are not a realistic target.
  • Don't crank retraction — over-retraction with PETG jams and blobs. The Bambu profiles are already tuned.
  • Textured PEI only — PETG fuses to smooth PEI plates.
03b

PETG stringing is a failure chain, not one defect

Every mode below comes from the same two material properties: PETG stays tacky and stretchy in the melt far longer than PLA, and it is hygroscopic. Ordered from cosmetic to print-killing:

#ModeMechanismWhere it bites
1Classic stringing (hairs)Residual melt pressure oozes during travel; PLA ooze snaps, PETG ooze stretches like hot mozzarella and draws a hair across the travel pathMulti-island prints maximize travels; on interlocking toys the hairs accumulate inside the clearance gaps
2Moisture fuzzWater flashes to steam in the nozzle: faint popping, foamy inconsistent extrusion, rough matte surfaces, cobwebs everywhere, weaker layersA spool left out a couple of humid weeks. If it "used to print fine," it's moisture, not settings
3Blobs & seam defectsMelt tack keeps the nozzle from releasing cleanly at perimeter starts/stops → raised seam blobsCosmetic on display parts; on a mating surface a seam blob is a hard bump inside your clearance — hence seams Aligned/Rear for print-in-place
4Nozzle boogersWiped-back ooze clings to the hot nozzle and carbonizes; blobs let go into the printMystery brown specks in a clean part; big accretions drag through top surfaces. Silicone sock + occasional warm brass-brush wipe
5Strings become mechanical failuresA hair spanning a print-in-place gap gets welded over by the next layer → a tack weld between parts meant to move; string forests can also catch the nozzle and lift small islandsFused or gritty mechanisms; stringing escalating into knock-over / adhesion failures
6The cooling dilemma (curl)PETG wants low fan for adhesion and gloss, but overhangs need fan to not curl and droop; profiles split the differencePETG overhangs always trail PLA's; curled overhang edges are nozzle-strike bait on tall parts

Taming it on the P2S, in order of impact — most of this is already in Bambu's PETG profiles:

  • Dry filament — nothing else matters if it's wet. The AMS with fresh desiccant keeps a dry spool dry; it does not dry a wet one.
  • Temperature at the low end — ooze rate scales strongly with temp; drop 10 °C before touching anything else.
  • Avoid crossing walls + z-hop both on — fewer travels over open space, and the hop snaps strings instead of dragging them.
  • Leave retraction alone — the Bambu profiles are tuned; cranking it causes jams and worse blobs.
  • Buy PETG HF — formulated to print faster with less of all of the above than Generic/Basic PETG.

05

3MF project files are printer-specific

A downloaded 3MF carries the author's whole machine context. Before printing someone else's project — or shipping your own — know what's baked in:

  • Printer preset (printer_settings_id, printer_model): the print dialog refuses a mismatched machine. Fix on the Prepare page — switch printer, re-slice. The listed process/filament presets are also machine-suffixed (@BBL A1 vs @BBL P2S).
  • Exclusion zones (bed_exclude_area): the P1S draws an 18×28 mm no-print corner; the P2S has none. They travel with the machine preset, not the model.
  • Prime tower position (wipe_tower_x/y): stored as absolute coordinates — can hang off a different printer's usable area. Drag it toward the middle if the slicer complains.
  • Arrangement: object spacing was validated against the author's toolhead clearance. After any printer switch, re-Arrange — especially in by-object mode (P2S wants 72 mm; P1S only needed 57 mm).
  • Filament list ≠ filament used: slots can be leftovers. Check Metadata/slice_info.config for what the plate actually consumes before buying anything.
  • Unsolicited settings: imported projects carry the author's supports, infill, and sequence choices — review print_sequence, enable_support, brim_type rather than trusting them.
Generating your own 3MFs: the high-leverage keys in Metadata/project_settings.config are printer_settings_id, curr_bed_type, print_sequence, brim_type/brim_width, wall_loops, enable_support, wipe_tower_x/y, and the per-object extruder assignments in model_settings.config. Set those per job from the recipes above and a file slices cleanly on the machine it claims to target.
Headless / CLI slicing traps (if scripting against the BambuStudio CLI): preset inherits chains are not resolved — flatten them or you silently get parent defaults; always name the plate via curr_bed_type (the Cool Plate fallback can't slice PETG at all); verify by reading layer_height back out of the G-code.
Numbers cited (1h30m vs 16h42m, clearance radii, thresholds) were measured on this P2S with BambuStudio's own slicer. Recipes assume the 0.20 mm Standard profile as the base. Mirrored from the "P2S Field Guide" artifact into the Fidget Shelf app.

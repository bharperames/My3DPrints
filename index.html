Reading the numbers

Ø means diameter — the full width of a circle through its center, in millimeters. "Ø19 ball" is a ball 19 mm across.

Every figure on a card is measured from the mesh or read out of a real slice, and print notes record what happened on the printer.

P2S Field Guide →

Slice figures come from real Bambu Studio CLI runs (0.2 mm Standard, PLA Basic, textured PEI, 15% infill). Calibrated against the printer: masses land within ±6%, times read 10–25% low and drift further on long prints.

Cross-design compatibility

Two pieces mate when their interface genus (slot or thread count) and profile radii match — diameters alone are not enough.

  • V2 passthrough series (designer-stated, measured)Mini Fidget Ball and Mini Stackable share the same canonical ball mesh at 13.5 / 27 / 39 mm — the 27 mm ball threads both the fidget disc (Ø34.9) and the stackable tube (Ø35.4). Stackable sections thread into each other indefinitely: male passes female by design.
  • Passthrough balls on the sphere standsA ball sits on a stand when its Ø exceeds the rim Ø. The 27 mm ball rests on the 1″ stand (rim 19.2 mm, contact ≈45°) and the 39 mm ball on the 2″ stand (rim 36.5 mm — deep seat, ≈69°). The 13.5 mm ball falls through every stand.
  • Staryu vs Starmie — same design, no shared partsStaryu is the same spinner at 94% scale: pins, spacers and heads do not interchange between the two (designer-confirmed, and the pin Øs differ). The diff_disc washer stack is the shared tuning hardware within each spinner.
  • Hourglass cone & pyramid share one 7-lobe thread (measured)Cross-sections show every hourglass part rides the same 7-lobe inner mechanism — cone and pyramid differ only in outer skin (round vs square). Clearance measured 0.38 mm for all four solid×spiral combinations at the mid-connection, so same-size cone and pyramid parts likely interchange. Size classes do not mix: small and dubbel waist profiles differ.
  • Spinning top connector is fit-criticalThe two connectors differ by 0.06–0.07 mm for different filaments; the designer warns any rescale of the top breaks the fit, since clearance does not scale with the part.

File ledger

Genus is the count of tunnels passing clear through a closed solid — a ball is genus 0, a donut genus 1, and each extra handle or helical slot adds one. It is computed from the mesh itself (Euler’s formula: vertices − edges + faces = 2 − 2·genus), so it fingerprints the interlock mechanism no matter how the surface is decorated: the passthrough ball’s 21 helical channels read out as genus 21, and the hourglass bodies’ slot counts as genus 8–19.

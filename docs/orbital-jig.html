What is drawn is what prints. The bodies are read straight out of the 3MF the shop builds for this part — the file that goes to the printer — and put back into their assembled positions from the transforms the generator wrote into the same archive. Nothing here is a copy of the geometry made for the page. The grey parts are stand-ins for hardware that is bought, not printed: three 608 bearings on their M4 axles, the M5 thumbscrew, the ballast, and the camera you pick: a phone held landscape in a spring clamp on the receiver's 1/4-20, or a Sony α1 on an Arca plate behind one of its macros, the receiver slid along its channel to sit under the body's socket. The main lens is on the 150 mm sphere either way. The specimen is the scanned tooth from models/specimen/, at its real size, base on the platform; the jig is built with its focal height at that tooth's centroid. ?file= picks another glTF from that folder and ?specimen= overrides the height.

The sequence is the spec's state machine. Set elevation along the arc, turn the rotor to the next azimuth, hold 200 ms for the mechanics to settle, expose — the frustum flashes at 20 to 200 mm and a ray is cast to the specimen — then increment. Four rings, 108 frames. The viewfinder inset is a second render from the camera's own position with the chosen lens's field, so each flash shows what that frame holds. The spec drew one 32° frustum, which frames 86 × 57 mm at 150 mm and cannot hold a 129 mm tooth; the phone's main camera frames 216 × 162, and the α1's 90 macro 60 × 40, so the lens is a choice here. What no lens fixes is depth of field: a phone's main camera at f/1.8 and 150 mm holds roughly 5 mm in focus against a 31 mm-thick specimen, where the α1's macro at f/8–f/13 holds the whole tooth.

One thing the spec does not add up on. The rotor's detent ring has 36 dimples at 10°, which serves the horizon ring (10° steps) and the zenith ring (20°). The 12° and 15° steps of the middle rings land between dimples: on those the thumb holds the rotor against the pawl's spring, and the HUD says so.
